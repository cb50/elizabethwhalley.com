![Oiseaux Morin-Heights Birds project promotion](@assets/oiseaux-morin-heights-birds/1-promo.jpg)

An art and science project about the birds of Morin-Heights, a municipality in the Laurentian region of Quebec.

Goal: to explore, celebrate, and help the birds of Morin-Heights through education, creation, and local activism.

A Facebook group was created for the project to post all the events.

[<u>https://www.facebook.com/groups/1215020710013143</u>](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1215020710013143)

After a yearlong schedule of workshops, outings, and lectures, the participants’ projects were presented in an exhibition at the Chalet Bellevue in Morin-Heights. <section class="project-section" aria-label="Kildeer">

### Kildeer

<figure class="project-figure">

![Kildeer](@assets/oiseaux-morin-heights-birds/7-killdeer.jpg)

<figcaption>Kildeer</figcaption>

</figure>

I would walk through Watchorn’s fields, stretched out at the foot of our property, to the old school when I was in third grade.

Kildeer liked to hunt insects and worms in the stubbly fields.

Killdeer often live near people, making them vulnerable to pesticide poisoning and collisions with cars and buildings.

</section>

<section class="project-section" aria-label="Bank swallow">

### Bank swallow

<figure class="project-figure">

![Bank swallow](@assets/oiseaux-morin-heights-birds/4-bank-swallow.jpg)

<figcaption>Bank swallow</figcaption>

</figure>

During summer vacation we explored the hillsides above Watchorn’s farm and slid down the sides of the gravel pits. Bank swallows darted overhead catching insects.

A highway and residential roads now cut across the hillsides. The gravel pits have closed, trees have grown in, houses have appeared.

Threats to the rapidly disappearing bank swallow include loss of nesting habitat of vertical sand or mud banks, bluffs and gravel pits, loss of open foraging habitat, collision with vehicles, pesticide killing their food, and impacts of climate change.

</section>

<section class="project-section" aria-label="Kingfisher">

### Kingfisher

<figure class="project-figure">

![Kingfisher](@assets/oiseaux-morin-heights-birds/5-kingfisher.jpg)

<figcaption>Kingfisher</figcaption>

</figure>

At the Bennet Road bridge we would clamber down the banks of the stream and hop on the rocks upstream and downstream, the light filtering through the overhanging trees that surrounded and sheltered it. In the quiet, a kingfisher would sometimes perch then dive to catch a minnow.

Now there’s a road beside the stream with houses.

The belted kingfisher is sensitive to disturbance and may abandon his territory if people begin frequenting it.

</section>

<section class="project-section" aria-label="Barred owl">

### Barred owl

<figure class="project-figure">

![Barred owl](@assets/oiseaux-morin-heights-birds/6-barred-owl.jpg)

<figcaption>Barred owl</figcaption>

</figure>

The dazzling luminescence of ski slopes can disrupt owl hunting and migration behaviors.

At night in the winter, from behind our house, as a child, I could see the black shapes of the hills across the valley where the barred owls would hoot in the darkness then silently hunt their prey.

The dazzling luminescence from ski hills can disrupt the hunting and migratory behaviours of owls.

</section>
